# Shu Hero Art Direction

**Project:** War of Three Kingdoms (WTK)  
**Faction:** Shu  
**Purpose:** Unified visual specification for Shu hero illustrations and future image-generation/editing work  
**Reference basis:** the approved Shu cavalry artwork supplied on 2026-10-01, plus the established Shu hero design rules from the WTK art direction.

> The faction must read immediately as **ancient parchment yellow + Shu bamboo/teal green**.  
> Individual hero colours are secondary and must never replace the faction identity.

---

## 1. Core Visual Formula

**Shu hero = warm ancient parchment × bamboo/teal-green faction identity × pale ink-wash Shu landscape × historically distinctive hero silhouette × restrained bronze/gold × battle-worn Han-era detail × strong but controlled Shu banner.**

The first glance should say **“this is a Shu card.”**  
The second glance should say **“this is unmistakably this hero.”**

The overall mood is:

- Han legitimacy
- righteousness and loyalty
- resilience after hardship
- mountains, rivers and fortified passes
- practical military strength rather than fantasy spectacle
- an old historical painting preserved on a thousand-year scroll

---

## 2. Extracted Colour Scheme from the Approved Reference

The values below are approximate representative colours sampled from the approved reference artwork. They are intended as production anchors rather than pixel-perfect restrictions because lighting, texture and ageing effects will shift local values.

| Role | Colour | Hex | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parchment highlight | warm ivory parchment | `#F4D3B4` | brightest paper, robe highlights, horse highlights |
| Aged silk / paper | warm ochre beige | `#E3B081` | main background field |
| Historical wash | muted tan | `#BF9C7F` | mist, distant landscape, aged textile |
| Ochre accent | dry golden ochre | `#D99E68` | old paper variation, dust, restrained warmth |
| Bronze brown | antique bronze leather | `#99663E` | belts, saddlery, borders, armour ornament |
| Shu bamboo green | desaturated dark green | `#455144` | principal faction banner, cloth accents |
| Deep Shu green | deep weathered green | `#323C32` | banner shadow, distant Shu flags, dark textile |
| Ink charcoal | blue-black charcoal | `#27323B` | armour shadow, weapons, linework, distant troops |
| Hero blue accent | muted steel blue | `#3B5F93` | optional character-specific accent only |
| Deep blue accent | dark slate blue | `#304464` | secondary folds, spear wrapping, cool metal contrast |
| Warm dark brown | aged wood / border | `#4E2915` | frame, poles, wood, deep warm shadow |
| Near-black ink | warm black | `#1F110B` | deepest edge, hair, border distress |

### Colour hierarchy

Use the palette in this priority order:

1. **Warm ancient parchment** — dominant field.
2. **Shu bamboo/teal green** — mandatory faction anchor.
3. **Neutral ink and aged bronze** — structural detail.
4. **Hero-specific colour** — limited accent.
5. **Han red** — very small emphasis only.

A useful target balance is approximately:

- 45–55% warm parchment / beige / ochre
- 15–25% Shu bamboo or teal green
- 10–20% neutral ink, weathered metal and bronze
- 5–15% hero-specific accent
- under 5% strong red or bright metallic highlights

These percentages are visual guidance, not strict rendering metrics.

### Important note on the blue in the reference

The approved cavalry reference contains a strong blue accent. Treat this as a **character-specific colour**, not the Shu faction colour.

The faction layer must remain **ancient-paper yellow + bamboo/teal green** even when a hero has blue, white, red, black, silver or another personal colour.

## 3. Mandatory Shu Faction Identity

Every Shu hero artwork must retain all four elements below.

### 3.1 Ancient-paper foundation

Use warm beige, old silk yellow, faded ochre and visible paper fibre.

The surface should show:

- age stains
- faded pigment
- dry-brush wear
- subtle water marks
- mottling
- historical print / rubbing texture

Do not use a clean flat yellow digital background.

### 3.2 Bamboo/teal-green faction anchor

A clearly visible desaturated green must appear in the composition.

Preferred range:

- `#455144`
- `#3C473C`
- `#323C32`

It can appear on:

- the main Shu command banner
- secondary unit flags
- robe trim
- cape lining
- headcloth
- military textiles

Do not tint the entire artwork green.

### 3.3 Shu emblem

A visible **“蜀”** faction mark is mandatory.

The current approved standard is a **large principal Shu command banner** rather than a small hidden flag.

Recommended placement:

- upper-left or upper-right rear third of the composition
- behind the hero, never directly over the face
- large enough to remain readable when the card is displayed at small size
- accompanied by a few smaller matching banners in the distant army when useful

The principal banner should use:

- dark bamboo/teal-green cloth
- warm pale-gold or parchment-coloured “蜀”
- restrained geometric / cloud-thunder border ornament
- torn or weathered cloth edges where appropriate

The banner may be visually substantial, but the **hero must remain the primary focal point**.

The “蜀” emblem is the intentional exception to the general no-text rule. Do not add random Chinese writing, poems, labels or decorative pseudo-calligraphy elsewhere.

### 3.4 Pale historical background

Mountains, rivers, city walls, passes, camps, ships, formations and soldiers should look like historical memory absorbed into the paper.

Use:

- pale gray-black ink
- pale ochre ink
- faded green on Shu banners
- low-to-medium contrast

The distant world should support the hero rather than compete with the hero.

---

## 4. Character Hierarchy

The character should occupy roughly **70–80% of the card height** and remain the clearest object in the image.

Keep the area immediately behind the head relatively simple.

The pose should express the hero's identity:

- **warriors:** active weapon technique, command, charge, defence or pursuit
- **strategists:** planning, observing, calculating, reading terrain, directing formations
- **rulers:** command presence, long-distance gaze, judgement and authority
- **noblewomen / royal figures:** dignified posture, controlled gestures, political or household authority where appropriate

Avoid repeating the same frontal three-quarter standing pose across the whole faction.

Use:

- lateral movement
- diagonal movement
- body twist
- over-the-shoulder direction
- mounted command
- defensive stance
- weapon recovery
- pointing / ordering gestures
- calm strategic stillness

## 5. Shu Clothing and Material Language

### Shared faction materials

Preferred materials:

- aged silk
- woven Han textile
- dark leather
- old iron
- red bronze
- muted gold
- carved wood
- bamboo
- jade in small amounts

Preferred faction colours:

- bamboo green
- desaturated teal-green
- plain white / moon white
- Han red
- old bronze
- warm dark leather

### Pattern vocabulary

Use subtle patterns derived from:

- Han cloud-thunder motifs
- auspicious cloud forms
- bronze vessel geometry
- kui patterns
- pictorial-brick linework
- Shu brocade geometry
- bamboo-leaf forms
- bamboo-slip rhythm
- Han textile borders

These should feel:

- faded
- woven
- rubbed
- engraved
- stamped
- partially worn away

They must not look like modern logos or luminous game UI.

---

## 6. Border Standard

The Shu cards should use a shared collectible-card frame language.

Combine:

- Han bronze inlay
- ancient wooden window-lattice geometry
- cloud-thunder motifs
- kui-dragon derived forms
- roof-tile / architectural corner motifs
- distressed antique finish

The border should be solemn and archaeological, not a bright fantasy rarity frame.

Avoid:

- glowing SSR-style borders
- oversized gold
- neon
- magical gemstones
- overly symmetrical modern UI decoration

---

## 7. Background Vocabulary for Shu

Preferred Shu environments include:

- Shu mountain roads
- layered Sichuan mountains
- mist and river valleys
- Jingzhou routes
- Yizhou fortifications
- Hanzhong passes
- river crossings
- military camps
- marching formations
- ships
- civilians under protection
- watchtowers
- fortified mountain gates

All background storytelling remains subordinate to the hero.

Character-specific stories may influence local detail, but they must not replace the faction template.

Examples:

- Ma Chao may suggest Western Liang dust and cavalry, but the image still sits in the Shu parchment-and-green system.
- Zhao Yun may use silver-white armour and muted blue accents, but the faction colour remains Shu green.
- Guan Yu may use deep green robes and Han red, but the battlefield must not become a saturated green fantasy scene.
- Huang Yueying may use maps, bamboo slips and planning instruments, but the scene should not become a workshop illustration.

---

## 8. Warrior Design Rules

Shu warriors should feel practical, loyal, battle-tested and historically grounded.

Use:

- red-bronze or old-iron armour
- leather
- bamboo-green robes or faction details
- plain white cloth
- restrained red accents
- command seals
- tiger tallies
- military banners
- drums
- quivers
- capes
- historically plausible weapons

Combat action must match the weapon.

### Weapon scale and anatomy

Weapons must feel functional and correctly scaled.

Check:

- spear length
- sword length
- blade mass
- grip position
- two-handed vs one-handed logic
- scabbard location
- horse tack
- shoulder–elbow–wrist relationship
- finger placement
- weapon / body intersections

No floating weapons, impossible grips or toy-sized polearms.

---

## 9. Strategist and Civil-Official Rules

Strategists should feel intelligent through behaviour and environment, not magical effects.

Preferred colours:

- moon white
- bamboo green
- gray-green
- light brown
- muted blue-green where character-appropriate

Preferred objects:

- bamboo slips
- maps
- seals
- chess / strategy pieces
- scrolls
- writing brush
- command flags
- campaign tables

Avoid:

- glowing magic circles
- floating runes
- supernatural energy
- excessive particle effects

A strategist's intelligence should be visible in expression, posture and battlefield organisation.

---

## 10. Rulers and High-Rank Identity

Imperial motifs are hierarchical.

Appropriate for rulers or very high status only:

- restrained coiling-dragon or kui-dragon references
- imperial seals
- ceremonial crown elements
- formal symbolic textile patterns

Ordinary officers should rely on:

- tiger tallies
- military seals
- banners
- drums
- weapons
- armour patterns
- rank-appropriate headwear

Do not give every Shu character imperial dragon decoration.

---

## 11. Historical Character Distinction

Uniformity comes from:

- parchment
- faction green
- banner treatment
- border language
- ink-wash background
- Han-era motif system

Individuality comes from:

- face shape
- age
- beard
- body proportions
- headwear
- armour construction
- weapon
- pose
- personal accent colour
- historically or literarily distinctive event

Do not beautify away a character's unusual recorded traits.

Translate distinctive descriptions visually and respectfully.

The goal is not generic attractiveness. The goal is recognisable historical-literary identity.

## 12. Current Character-Specific Notes

### Liu Bei

Core identity:

- benevolent but politically formidable ruler
- Han legitimacy
- resilience after repeated setbacks

Visual anchors:

- bamboo green
- plain white
- Han red cape
- red-bronze armour
- twin kingly swords with believable carry / draw logic
- ruler-level headwear
- longer arms and distinctive ears interpreted tastefully

### Guan Yu

Core identity:

- solemn loyalty
- intimidating battlefield authority

Visual anchors:

- deep green robe
- long beard
- heavy guandao
- red accents
- large, controlled silhouette

Avoid turning him into a bright green-and-gold fantasy warrior.

### Zhang Fei

Core identity:

- explosive force
- rough battlefield energy
- intimidating physical mass

Visual anchors:

- darker iron / brown armour
- Han red cloth
- powerful forward action
- heavy practical polearm

Avoid purple magic, dark-fantasy energy or supernatural effects.

### Zhao Yun

Core identity:

- calm elite warrior
- loyal protector
- fast, precise battlefield control

Visual anchors from the approved reference:

- silver-white armour
- white horse
- white / moon-white cloth
- optional muted steel-blue personal accent
- long spear
- clean young heroic face
- Shu-green banners remain the faction anchor

### Ma Chao

Core identity:

- “Brocade Ma Chao”
- Western Liang cavalry power
- pride, vengeance and later loyalty

Visual formula:

**jade-like face × silver lion armour × white brocade × white horse × long spear × aggressive command / pursuit × Western Liang cavalry × subtle Tong Pass narrative.**

Preferred action should differ from Zhao Yun:

- stronger aggression
- pointing / command gesture
- pursuit
- torso rotation
- lateral or diagonal horse movement

### Zhuge Liang

Core identity:

- transcendent strategist without literal magic
- absolute strategic control
- loyalty and endurance

Visual anchors:

- moon white
- blue-green / bamboo green
- white feather fan
- bamboo slips or command material
- scholar's headcloth
- restrained jade
- calm mountain-and-army backdrop

### Huang Yueying

Core identity:

- strategic intelligence
- unconventional appearance
- resilience and competence

Visual anchors:

- relatively darker complexion
- not idealised into generic beauty
- brush as a strong personal prop
- maps and military planning
- bamboo slips
- strategic environment

Do not turn her into a generic workshop craftswoman.

### Gan Furen

Core identity:

- dignified Shu noblewoman / royal household presence
- grace with political and maternal authority

Visual anchors:

- elegant Han feminine silhouette
- CG / painterly stylisation rather than photorealism
- controlled expression
- refined Shu green, cream and Han red
- dignified hand posture

For a “mother of the realm” feeling, hands may be gently crossed and layered in front of the waist, with a stable upright posture and minimal gestural noise.

## 13. Strict Exclusions

Do not use:

- pure black backgrounds
- dark fantasy treatment
- Western fantasy armour language
- cyberpunk
- neon
- modern UI
- saturated blue skies
- saturated red skies
- giant glowing gold
- magic circles
- energy beams
- particle explosions
- excessive lens effects
- random text
- fake Chinese characters
- background contrast stronger than the hero
- repeated generic poses across every hero
- tiny faction marks that disappear at card size

---

## 14. Production Layer Priority

When requirements conflict, use this order:

### Layer 1 — Shu master template

Cannot be overridden:

- warm ancient parchment
- visible bamboo/teal-green faction anchor
- clear Shu emblem
- pale historical ink-wash environment
- common antique border language

### Layer 2 — Character identity

May customise:

- personal colours
- armour
- clothing
- weapon
- headwear
- physique
- pose
- horse
- individual motif

### Layer 3 — Story scene

May customise:

- location
- troops
- campaign reference
- city
- river
- pass
- ships
- civilians
- narrative event

This layer must have the lowest visual priority.

---

## 15. Card-Size Readability Test

Before approving a Shu hero image, reduce it to approximately card-display size and check:

- Is the hero immediately readable?
- Is the “蜀” emblem still obvious?
- Does the card still read as parchment yellow + Shu green?
- Is the hero silhouette distinct from other Shu heroes?
- Is the weapon understandable?
- Are the face and hands readable?
- Does the background remain secondary?
- Does the border still look antique rather than noisy?

If the Shu identity disappears at thumbnail size, the banner or faction-green structure is too weak.

If the banner becomes the first and only thing seen, reduce its contrast or move it deeper into the composition without making it too small.

## 16. Reusable Image Prompt Skeleton

```text
Create a War of Three Kingdoms Shu hero card in a unified ancient Chinese historical-scroll style.

MASTER FACTION TEMPLATE:
- warm thousand-year parchment / aged silk background
- visible paper fibres, stains, faded pigment and dry-brush wear
- dominant warm beige / ochre paper field
- mandatory desaturated bamboo/teal-green Shu faction colour
- one clearly readable principal Shu command banner bearing the single character “蜀”
- principal banner positioned in the rear upper-left or upper-right third, large enough to read at card size but subordinate to the hero
- optional smaller matching Shu flags in the distant formation
- pale gray-black and ochre ink-wash mountains, rivers, cities, camps or troops
- antique Han bronze / wooden lattice / cloud-thunder border
- no other text

CHARACTER:
[hero-specific face, age, physique, clothing, armour, weapon, headwear and pose]

CHARACTER COLOUR:
[personal accent colours; these must not replace the Shu green faction layer]

STORY:
[one historically distinctive event or location, rendered subtly in the distant background]

COMPOSITION:
- hero is the sole primary focal point, approximately 70–80% of card height
- keep the area around the head relatively clean
- use a pose unique to this hero
- historically plausible weapon scale and hand anatomy
- restrained cinematic CG + Chinese ink-painting finish
- character core is clear; outer robe, hair and cape edges can dissolve into dry brush / flying-white / faded pigment

AVOID:
dark fantasy, neon, magic effects, modern UI, giant bright gold, saturated skies, random writing, fake Chinese text, floating weapons, impossible anatomy, generic repeated poses, tiny Shu emblem.
```

---

## 17. Final Approval Rule

A successful Shu card should satisfy this sentence:

> **From across the screen it belongs to the same Shu army; up close it could only be this one hero.**

The approved faction signature is:

**warm ancient paper + large readable weathered Shu-green banner + restrained bronze + pale ink history + character-specific identity.**
