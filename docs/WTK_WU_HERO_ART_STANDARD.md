# WTK Character Artwork Standard — Wu Faction

## Core identity
Han–Three Kingdoms historical scroll × aged xuan paper × vermilion ink wash × Jiangdong military/naval character × aged bronze Han motifs × collectible character card.

Keywords: vermilion, Jiangdong, river mist, martial elegance, naval power, aged bronze, historical scroll.

Faction backgrounds: Shu = pale warm yellow; Wei = pale black/ink-grey; Wu = pale-to-medium vermilion/red ink wash. Wu red belongs to the world/background; not every Wu character wears red.

## Wu background — highest-priority rule
Begin with warm aged xuan paper (beige, ochre-yellow, parchment), then wash a clearly visible field of cinnabar/vermilion, aged scarlet, red-brown, dark crimson and burnt vermilion into it. Red must look like pigment absorbed by old paper, never a flat fill or digital filter.

Use uneven ink density, water diffusion, dry brush/flying-white texture, exposed parchment, faded pigment, worn edges, stains and paper absorption.

The approved Lu Xun revision establishes the baseline: medium vermilion, immediately identifiable as Wu while warm parchment still breathes.

Approximate palette targets:
- Main vermilion/red-brown: #8F2F25–#A63A2B
- Deep smoky red: #542B28–#6D2924
- Burnt vermilion highlight: #C65332–#D86A3D
- Warm parchment: about #D6A36B

Use these as visual targets, not rigid digital fills. Suggested background balance: red family 55–65%, parchment/ochre 20–25%, smoky dark ink 15–20%. Avoid pale pink/orange and near-black wine-red extremes.

Acceptance test: hide the character and faction glyph. The remaining background should still read immediately as Wu.

## The “吴” faction glyph
One large “吴” may appear as a background identifier. Preferred colours: aged parchment-gold, pale ochre-gold, faded ivory or light aged bronze. The approved direction is aged parchment-gold/pale ochre-gold.

Treat it as ancient brush calligraphy or a faded rubbing: broken edges, absorbed pigment, low-to-medium contrast. Never use red-on-red, modern fonts, 3D lettering, metallic logo rendering, glow, neon, bevel or UI badges.

No other text should normally appear. Do not add character names, random Chinese characters, pseudo-Chinese writing or text on banners.

## Character colour
Background unifies the faction; the character carries individual identity. Wu characters may use navy/indigo, ink-blue, smoky blue, ivory/moon-white, black iron, dark brown, muted purple, bronze, aged scarlet, red-brown or grey-white.

Do not turn the roster into repeated red armour + red cape + red banner designs.

## Historical costume and armour
Costume should read as late Han / Three Kingdoms Chinese with controlled heroic stylisation. Use lacquered lamellar armour, black iron, aged bronze, dark leather, restrained ochre-gold fittings, Han cloud/wave/kui/animal/geometric/tile-end motifs. Metals are aged, oxidised, worn and low-gloss.

Scholar-generals and strategists may use historically grounded robes, light armour, crowns/headgear, swords, seals, military orders, maps or bamboo documents. Women should use late-Han noble or martial clothing language rather than modern fantasy hanfu/xianxia or later-dynasty fashion.

Avoid bright luxury gold, European fantasy armour and Japanese Sengoku styling.

## Character differentiation
Design from the individual’s identity, rank, age, temperament, biography, military role, signature events and weapons. Commander, fierce warrior, veteran, young officer, scholar-general, strategist, naval commander, archer, ruler and noblewoman should have distinct silhouettes.

Do not merely swap faces or weapons. Pose, armour weight, costume, props and background narrative should change.

## Jiangdong and naval language
Wu can use rivers, banks, crossings, naval camps, tower ships, warships, masts, sails, docks, water gates, Jiangnan hills, Jiangdong cities, reeds and mist. Wu does not mean everyone stands on a ship; choose scenery from the character’s story.

Water remains secondary to the red faction field: pale ink lines, smoky blue-grey, grey ink, paper reserve and mist. Avoid bright blue oceans and modern red-vs-blue grading.

## Background storytelling
Use the individual’s history: fleets, camps, city walls, rivers, hills, formations, siege works, watchtowers, distant banners, smoke and controlled fire. Secondary figures remain small, pale ink silhouettes.

Fire attacks are historical storytelling, not modern VFX. Use vermilion/ochre ink, faded flame lines, smoke and distant burning areas. Avoid full-frame orange fire, explosions, particles and heavy cinematic black smoke overwhelming the portrait.

## Pose
Do not repeat one static pose. Derive action from identity: spear/blade, drawing a bow, hand on sword, command seal, issuing orders, observing formations, ship bow, enemy observation, advance or post-battle stance. The pose should communicate personality without the name.

## Weapons and props
Use historically/traditionally appropriate weapons. Long weapons retain convincing length and may leave the frame. Swords need coherent blade/guard/grip/scabbard relationships. Bows need plausible string, arrow and hand geometry.

Important props such as command seals, orders, bamboo documents, sword hilts and tally tokens must separate clearly from armour and read instantly.

Never allow floating weapons, body intersections, impossible scabbards, extra hilts, incorrect grips, malformed hands/fingers or decorative weapons with no physical source.

## Visual hierarchy
Strict order:
1. Face and eyes
2. Upper body and primary action
3. Weapon or important prop
4. Armour, clothing and identity details
5. “吴” glyph
6. Ships, city, army or historical event
7. Water, mountains, sky and distant scenery

If levels 5–7 compete with 1–4, reduce background contrast, saturation or detail. Keep head/shoulder silhouette clear by simplifying/lightening the background rather than adding modern rim light.

## Wu card border
Use the common WTK Han–Wei collectible-card language: Han bronze inlay motifs + ancient wooden lattice + cloud/thunder, kui and tile-end motifs.

Wu treatment: aged red-brown lacquer, corroded bronze, dark ochre-gold, very limited ink-blue. It must look historical and worn, never like a glowing mobile-game rarity/equipment frame.

## Negative constraints
Avoid: flat pure-red or near-black red backgrounds; pale pink Wu backgrounds; modern red filters; excessive bright orange fire; large fresh-red skies; identical red armour/capes; bright blue water/sky; modern cinematic red/blue grading; giant fantasy waves/ships; pirate aesthetics; Japanese Sengoku; European fantasy armour; xianxia styling; magic, energy waves, glowing skills, particle explosions; neon/modern UI; large bright-gold areas; modern clothing/weapons; bad weapon proportions; floating weapons; anatomical distortion/extra hands/bad fingers; random/pseudo-Chinese text; modern fonts; text-covered banners; background sharper or more dominant than the hero.

## Final acceptance standard
First read — the character: face, identity, pose and key weapon/prop dominate.

Second read — Wu: vermilion ink-washed parchment establishes the faction without relying on clothing.

Third read — the story: only then should the viewer discover river, fleet, city, formation, campaign or other biography.

> Warm ancient parchment underneath; vermilion ink wash defines Wu. Individual colours define the hero; Jiangdong history lives quietly in the background.

WTK shorthand: **Pale yellow identifies Shu. Pale ink-black identifies Wei. Vermilion identifies Wu.**
