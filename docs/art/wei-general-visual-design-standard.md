# WTK — Wei General Visual Design Standard

## Purpose

This document defines the visual direction for **Wei (魏) generals** in *War of the Three Kingdoms*. It combines the shared WTK historical-character art language with the approved Wei-specific background and colour treatment.

The reference image supplied for this standard is the target for **overall Wei colour balance and atmosphere**, not a requirement to copy the depicted character, costume, pose, props, or battlefield.

---

## 1. Core Art Direction

**Han–Wei historical scroll × aged xuan paper and ink × Han artefact motifs × collectible character art**

Wei should feel severe, disciplined, calculating, militaristic, and northern. It must remain historical East Asian character art rather than modern high-saturation fantasy artwork.

Visual reading order:

1. The general
2. The Wei identity
3. The character's biography and historical setting hidden in the background

The character is always the primary visual focus.

---

## 2. Wei Colour Palette

The supplied reference is overwhelmingly dark and neutral. Approximate dominant colours extracted from the image are:

| Role | Approx. Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Near-black | `#0B0808` | deepest flags, shadows, foreground ink |
| Charcoal black | `#1E1819` | principal Wei background |
| Warm charcoal | `#30292C` | robes, smoke, secondary background |
| Iron grey | `#3C3D48` | armour, walls, distant atmosphere |
| Muted grey-brown | `#5D5457` | stone, smoke transitions |
| Dark bronze-brown | `#523D33` | leather, aged metal, warm shadow |
| Weathered grey | `#786D6E` | mid-distance highlights |
| Dusty taupe | `#9D8984` | restrained light areas |
| Faded parchment highlight | `#BFABA2` | limited warm highlights |
| Pale parchment | `#E8DBD0` | very limited highest highlights |

### Palette rule

**Wei backgrounds are black-led, not yellow-led.**

Use pale black, charcoal, soot grey, iron grey and black-brown as the dominant field. Warm aged-paper tones should survive only as restrained undertones and highlights.

Purple, dark indigo, muted red, aged bronze and ochre-gold may distinguish individual characters, but they must remain secondary.

Avoid large areas of bright gold, vivid blue, saturated red, or clean white.

---

## 3. Wei Background Standard

The approved Wei background language is:

**dark charcoal historical scroll + black ink atmosphere + grey historical scenery + restrained aged-paper undertone**

The background may include, when appropriate to the character:

- northern plains and mountains
- fortified cities and gates
- camps and military formations
- cavalry and infantry
- rivers and waterways
- siege structures
- watchtowers
- smoke, dust and weather
- military banners

These elements must remain lower-contrast and less detailed than the principal character.

The background should not become a separate landscape painting.

### Character separation

A dark Wei background must never swallow the silhouette.

Keep the immediate area around the face and important gesture sufficiently controlled in value and detail. If character and background merge, first simplify or lighten the local background rather than adding modern rim lighting or fantasy glow.

---

## 4. Wei Faction Mark

A large **魏** character is the principal faction identifier.

It may appear as:

- faded ink
- ancient rubbing
- weathered banner mark
- damaged seal-like impression
- aged bronze/ochre pigment on dark cloth

The principal 魏 mark should be large enough to read immediately as a faction symbol, while remaining behind the character in visual hierarchy.

Do not scatter unnecessary text across armour, weapons and scenery. Apart from the intentional Wei faction mark, character artwork should normally contain no names, titles, poems, random Chinese characters, pseudo-Chinese, or modern typography.

---

## 5. Character Composition

The general should occupy approximately **70–80% of the image height**.

Centred and slightly offset compositions are both acceptable. Frontal, profile, three-quarter, back-facing/looking-back and action poses are allowed.

Keep the area immediately behind the head relatively controlled. Avoid strong towers, mountain peaks, banners or high-contrast shapes cutting through the facial silhouette.

Place secondary troops, architecture and narrative elements toward the sides, lower body and distance.

---

## 6. Wei Armour and Costume Language

Wei military characters favour:

- black iron
- dark iron-grey
- black-brown lacquer
- aged bronze
- restrained ochre-gold
- dark indigo or muted purple
- small dark-red accents where character-appropriate

Decoration can draw from:

- cloud-and-thunder patterns
- kui motifs
- Han bronze-vessel ornament
- pictorial-brick linework
- roof-tile motifs
- ancient timber architecture

Metal should feel **black iron + thousand-year-old bronze**, not glossy black-and-gold luxury fantasy armour.

Not every Wei character wears heavy armour. Strategists, civil officials, rulers and young officers must use clothing appropriate to identity and role.

---

## 7. Character Differentiation

Do not turn the Wei roster into the same black-armoured general with a different face.

Use role-specific visual language:

- **Commander:** complete armour, controlled cloak, command posture, formations.
- **Vanguard / fierce general:** stronger physicality, practical weapon emphasis, worn armour.
- **Veteran:** weathered equipment, scars, restrained posture.
- **Guard / elite officer:** disciplined, orderly armour construction.
- **Strategist / civil officer:** robes, maps, bamboo slips, seals, command documents; less heavy armour.
- **Young officer:** lighter, faster silhouette and equipment.

Personal colours may include iron blue, deep violet, dark red, grey-white, muted teal or aged bronze while remaining inside the Wei palette.

---

## 8. Pose and Historical Identity

Pose must communicate identity rather than merely look dramatic.

Suitable actions include:

- directing troops
- holding or drawing a historically appropriate weapon
- observing enemy formations
- studying a map
- holding a military order or bamboo slips
- standing at a city wall
- mounted command
- preparing to charge
- surveying a battlefield after combat

Historical or traditional identifying features should be preserved where useful: headgear, beard, physique, weapon, clothing silhouette and characteristic bearing.

Literary exaggeration should be translated into believable anatomy rather than fantasy mutation.

---

## 9. Weapons, Hands and Props

Weapons must be structurally plausible and appropriate to the character.

- Polearms retain convincing full-scale proportions; they may continue outside the frame.
- Swords require coherent blade, hilt and scabbard relationships.
- Dual weapons require clearly separate positions and grips.
- Props such as cups, scrolls, seals, command tokens and bamboo slips must be visually separated from armour.

Check hand count, fingers, grip, wrist/elbow/shoulder alignment, weapon attachment and scabbard placement.

No floating weapons, intersecting anatomy, impossible grips or decorative weapons without a physical source.

---

## 10. Ink and Material Treatment

The character is clearer than the background but remains part of the ancient-painting system.

Use:

- broken ink edges
- dry brush
- flying-white brush texture
- pigment loss
- faded fabric and metal
- ink diffusion
- paper absorption
- weathered surfaces

The centre of the figure may be more solid and detailed. Cloak edges, hems, hair and peripheral forms may dissolve gradually into ink, smoke and paper texture.

---

## 11. Border Language

If a collectible-card border is present, use:

**Han bronze inlay + ancient wooden lattice + cloud/thunder, kui and roof-tile motifs**

Wei variants may lean toward black-brown, old bronze and dark ochre-gold.

The border should feel archaeological and refined, never like a modern mobile-game rarity frame.

---

## 12. Negative Constraints

Avoid:

- yellow-dominant Wei backgrounds
- pure flat black backgrounds with no material depth
- character/background merging
- Western fantasy armour
- cyberpunk
- modern UI decoration
- neon
- glossy luxury black-and-gold styling
- large bright-gold surfaces
- saturated blue skies
- large saturated red skies
- dominant flames
- magical glow
- energy waves
- particle skill effects
- modern clothing or weapons
- implausible anatomy
- malformed hands
- floating or intersecting weapons
- random text or pseudo-Chinese
- background elements sharper than the protagonist

---

## 13. Final Wei Test

A successful Wei portrait should read as:

> **Black and charcoal establish Wei; aged paper remains underneath; iron-grey history recedes into the distance; the general remains the clearest and strongest element.**

At first glance: **the character**.  
At second glance: **Wei**.  
At third glance: **the character's history embedded in the ink-dark battlefield and Han–Wei visual language**.

The final artwork should feel like an ancient Wei-era character scroll reinterpreted as premium collectible art — not a modern fantasy-game poster.
